--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project establishes a mathematically grounded, reproducible framework for
extracting an exact representative sample of 20,000 rows from an insurance
dataset of 1,200,000 rows (`train.csv`, ~194 MB raw CSV, ~898 MB in-memory) and
conducting an exhaustive, publication-grade Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA).

was selected to preserve the authentic multivariate joint distributions, natural

duplicate records, or distorted marginal distributions.

2. SAMPLING STRATEGY, SELECTION & EMPIRICAL VALIDATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Sampling Strategy Comparison & Mathematical Justification
Three sampling paradigms were rigorously benchmarked with N = 1,200,000 and n = 20,000
(sampling fraction f = 20,000 / 1,200,000 = 1/60 = 1.6667%):

1. Simple Random Sampling Without Replacement (SRS-WOR) [SELECTED]:
   - Max Numerical Mean Deviation across all columns: 1.308% (Previous Claims)
   - Average Numerical Mean Deviation: 0.453%
   - Max Categorical Proportion Shift across all 33 strata: 0.582%
   - Max Missingness Prevalence Shift: 0.331%
   - Mean Absolute Correlation Matrix Difference: 0.00575
   - Theoretical Rationale: By the Glivenko-Cantelli theorem and the Law of Large
     Numbers, SRS-WOR guarantees uniform convergence of the empirical CDF to the
     population CDF. Because it conditions on no specific partition, it maintains
     unbiased joint multivariate dependencies, natural missingness co-occurrences,
     and the true covariance matrix without introducing conditional bias.

2. Categorical Stratified Sampling (Policy Type x Smoking Status, 6 strata):
   - Max Numerical Mean Deviation: 1.071%
   - Max Categorical Proportion Shift: 0.766% (Worse than SRS on unstratified cols)
   - Max Missingness Prevalence Shift: 0.346%
   - Critique: Stratifying on a small subset of categorical variables forced exact
     cell counts on those specific features, but resulted in higher random drift on
     unstratified features (e.g., Customer Feedback, Occupation, Marital Status).

3. Multivariable Stratified Sampling (Target Quintiles x Policy Type, 15 strata):
   - Max Numerical Mean Deviation: 1.176%
   - Max Categorical Proportion Shift: 0.834%
   - Max Missingness Prevalence Shift: 0.321%
   - Critique: Stratifying on the target variable artificially conditions on the
     dependent variable, which risks creating subtle survival or truncation artifacts
     in regression modeling and distorts the natural variance of the estimators.

Conclusion: Simple Random Sampling Without Replacement (SRS-WOR) with random seed 42
was selected as the definitively superior methodology.

Documented Sampling Limitations & Diagnostic Subset Guidance:
1. Expected Representation of Ultra-Rare Events:
   In any unbiased sample of fraction f = 1/60, events occurring with population frequency
   below 1 in 60,000 (0.00167%) have an expected sample frequency below 0.33 records.
   Specifically:
   - `Previous Claims = 7`: 58 occurrences in 1.2M -> Expected in 20k = 0.967 rows (Sample observed: 0).
   - `Previous Claims = 8`: 8 occurrences in 1.2M -> Expected in 20k = 0.133 rows (Sample observed: 1).
   - `Previous Claims = 9`: 1 occurrence in 1.2M -> Expected in 20k = 0.017 rows (Sample observed: 0).
   - `Vehicle Age` missing: 6 occurrences in 1.2M -> Expected in 20k = 0.10 rows (Sample observed: 0).
   - `Insurance Duration` missing: 1 occurrence in 1.2M -> Expected in 20k = 0.017 rows (Sample observed: 0).
2. Diagnostic Recommendation:
   The primary sample of 20,000 rows must remain unpolluted by artificial oversampling to
   ensure valid statistical inferences and true population generalization. However, for specialized
   actuarial edge-case stress testing (e.g. testing pricing algorithms on policyholders with 7-9 prior
   claims), actuaries should query a separate, dedicated "High-Claim Diagnostic Slice" extracted
   specifically from the population records where `Previous Claims >= 7`.

4. SYNTHESIS OF CORE BUSINESS & ACTUARIAL FINDINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Highly Flat Unconditional Marginal Demographics:
   Traditional demographic predictors (Gender, Location, Marital Status, Education, Smoking) exhibit
   remarkably flat unconditional univariate differences with Premium Amount. Actuaries and data scientists
   must not assume standard linear additive pricing equations; non-linear combinations and latent
   risk-score interactions govern this dataset.

2. Heavy Tail Risk in Financial Metrics:
   Both `Annual Income` and `Premium Amount` carry legitimate heavy right tails with 4-5% of observations
   falling outside standard Tukey IQR fences. Truncating or clipping these tails would introduce severe
   underestimation of loss costs and underwriting revenue.

3. The Danger of Listwise Deletion:
   67.7% of all policy records have at least one missing field. Any data pipeline that utilizes `dropna()`
   will discard two-thirds of the business and introduce severe selection bias, as missingness in features
   like `Marital Status` and `Customer Feedback` is correlated with higher premium amounts.

4. Synthetic Data Signatures:
   Uniform distributions in age, duration, and dependents, combined with identical 15:21:39 timestamps
   and $1.00 income floors, reflect synthetic generation artifacts (Kaggle playground synthesis).
